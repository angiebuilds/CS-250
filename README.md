## How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating “user stories” help with this?

I interpret user needs by first understanding what the user is trying to accomplish rather than immediately deciding how I think the program should work. Throughout the SNHU Travel project, I learned how important it is to ask questions when requirements are unclear instead of making assumptions. User stories help with this because they describe functionality from the user's perspective while connecting the requested feature to its purpose and value. Acceptance criteria can then provide measurable expectations for determining whether that need has actually been met. During the project, I encountered user stories that initially seemed clear but required additional clarification before they could be properly developed and tested. This experience showed me that user stories are not simply instructions to follow; they create a starting point for continued communication between the Product Owner, Developers, Testers, and stakeholders.

## How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?

I approach program development as an iterative process in which planning, development, testing, feedback, and improvement continually influence one another. One of the most important lessons I learned from this course is that having a plan does not mean that the plan should never change. The SNHU Travel project demonstrated this when its business requirements changed during development and existing functionality could be preserved while the product adapted to the new direction. In my future development work, I would especially like to incorporate Daily Scrums, backlog refinement, iterative testing, and Sprint Retrospectives. Daily communication can expose impediments and misunderstandings early, while refinement and testing help ensure that changing requirements remain understood throughout development. Retrospectives are equally important because completing a project does not mean there is nothing left to learn from how it was completed.

## What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?

Being a good team member in software development means taking responsibility for my own work without treating that work as though it exists separately from everyone else's. Software development requires communication, accountability, knowledge sharing, and a willingness to ask for or provide help when needed. I believe a strong team should avoid creating knowledge silos in which one person becomes the only individual capable of completing or understanding a particular part of the product. Instead, team members should communicate what they are doing, raise impediments early, remain receptive to feedback, and share information that can make the entire team stronger. My experience with the different Scrum roles also showed me that collaboration does not mean everyone performs the same job. Each role contributes a different perspective, but those perspectives continually influence one another. Ultimately, being a good team member means contributing my own knowledge while remaining willing to learn from others and recognizing that the success of the product belongs to the team rather than any one individual.
